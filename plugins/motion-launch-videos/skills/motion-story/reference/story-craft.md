# Story craft: the script, the ask and the post

Step 1. A story short lives or dies on two moments: the first line (does the viewer stay?) and the last card (do they answer?). Everything between keeps raising the stakes until both answers to the question look reasonable.

## The shape

| Part | Share of the film | What happens | Example (the demo, "Ten minutes") |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | first line, under 3 s | the situation at full stakes, in the second person, present tense | "You just robbed a bank." |
| Stakes | 2 or 3 scenes | what you have, what you stand to lose, a number on screen | three bags, $4.2 million |
| Plan | 1 scene | how it was meant to go: the viewer now knows what "wrong" looks like | the map: drive, cameras, motel at midnight |
| The wait | 2 or 3 scenes | the plan stalls; small concrete details; time on screen | the room, the clock, voicemail, counting bills |
| Twist | 1 or 2 scenes | new information that cuts both ways | the news: a suspect in custody |
| The split | 1 or 2 scenes | two sources, two instructions, both plausible | an unknown number says run; the partner says wait |
| The ask | the last card | the choice, said and shown, two answers | STAY or RUN? |

60 to 80 s is the sweet spot: long enough to make the choice hard, short enough to be watched to the end. 12 to 16 scenes, one beat each.

## Lines

- Second person, present tense, short: "You call him. Straight to voicemail." Under about 12 words a line, one to three lines a scene.
- Concrete over vague: a time (11:52), a sum ($4.2 million), a room number (14), a name (Marco). Numbers go on screen in a HUD card too.
- No narration of what the picture shows unless it adds a fact. "The phone buzzes" is fine; "You look at the phone, which is buzzing" is not.
- One new fact per line. The viewer is listening and reading at once.
- The last spoken line is the question, in the viewer's words: "So. Do you stay... or do you run?"
- Mark two or three words a scene for colour: `*red*` for danger and the twist, `+green+` for money and good news, `!amber!` for times and places. Marks are not spoken.
- Numbers the voice could misread get a spoken form: `{ text: 'You pull in at !11:52!.', speak: 'You pull in at eleven fifty two.' }`.

## The question

- Exactly two answers, each one word on its button: STAY / RUN, TRUST / LEAVE, TAKE / WALK, CALL / WAIT, SIGN / TEAR.
- Both must be defensible from what the film showed. Plant a reason for each: the news says a suspect is in custody (so maybe he talked: run), but the unknown number could be the police bluffing (so wait).
- Never answer it. A sequel can.

## Premises that work

Each is one line, a twist and a split. Keep them fictional and keep the method out.

- **The getaway** (the demo): the partner is late, two texts disagree.
- **The briefcase**: a stranger leaves a case of cash on your train seat and gets off; a man with an earpiece boards and looks at you.
- **The lottery**: you win $40 million; your best friend bought the ticket with you as a joke and does not remember.
- **The cabin**: snowed in with three strangers; the radio says one of four hikers escaped a prison van.
- **The offer**: your boss offers you a promotion if you sign that a colleague's mistake was yours; he will fire someone either way.
- **The wallet**: a wallet with $2,000 and a hospital appointment card; the address is two hours away, your flight leaves in three.
- **The key**: a hotel key card under your door for a room that, per the front desk, does not exist.

## The post

- One or two lines that restate the premise and the split, then the question with its answers and a pointing emoji: "STAY or RUN? 👇".
- Say it is fiction and the voice is AI ("Fiction. AI voice."), and use the platform's AI-content label where there is one.
- 3 to 5 hashtags: the genre (#crimestory #storytime #whatwouldyoudo), the format (#animation), one topic tag.
- Pin a first comment with your own two-word answer only if you want to seed the debate; never the "right" answer.

## Sequels

A film whose comments split close to 50/50 has a sequel in it: open with the majority's answer ("Most of you stayed. Here is what happened.") and end on a new split.
