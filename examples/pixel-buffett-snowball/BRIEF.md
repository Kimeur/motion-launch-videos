# Brief: Warren Buffett, from $0 to billions, a pixel history

Every word and number on screen traces to a row in "Facts". The subject is a real, living person, so the film says only what published sources say, credits them on screen, and says it is an unofficial fan animation.

## Deliverable

| | |
|---|---|
| Subject | Warren Buffett's life in ten stops, 1930 to 2026 (no product, no live site: the user's request is "draw me the history of Warren Buffett from 0 to billions") |
| Style | pixel ([motion-pixel](../../plugins/motion-launch-videos/skills/motion-pixel/SKILL.md)), drawn entirely in code: every sprite is built from rectangles, discs and lines in `dev/art.js` |
| Format | 1920 x 1080 (16:9) from a 320 x 180 canvas at x6 |
| Duration | 36 s, not a loop (a story that plays once; it loops when a platform loops it) |
| Frame rate | 60 fps, sprites and the line on the 12-a-second grid |
| Tempo grid | 120 BPM: a beat is 0.5 s, a bar 2 s. The stops land every 3 s (6 beats), the music is a waltz in 3/4 |
| Sound | The picture works muted. An original chiptune waltz (`dev/music.mjs`) is muxed into the delivered MP4 |
| Where it plays | X / social feed |
| Deliverables | MP4 (H.264, BT.709) with sound, preview GIF, poster PNG, the single HTML file |

## Not on screen

- **Buffett's likeness.** The runner is a generic original sprite (a kid, a paper boy, a student, a man in a suit, an older man), identified only by the captions. No photo, no portrait, no one else's character.
- **Logos and covers.** No Forbes, CNBC, PBS or Berkshire logo, and no real book cover: the book is a plain navy book with a light bulb.
- **The 2026 changes at Berkshire** (Greg Abel became CEO on January 1, 2026; Buffett became chairman emeritus on September 18, 2026, with his son Howard as chairman). They are recent, the film is about how the snowball grew, and no row here needs them.
- **Berkshire's $1 trillion market value (August 28, 2024).** It is the company's value, not his net worth, and would blur the ladder of numbers.
- **Net worth for 1990 ($3.3B), 1995 ($10.7B) and 1999 ($36B),** which came only from aggregator pages, and **the farm he bought at 15 ($1,200 for 40 acres),** which came from one summary. Both are true as far as the summaries go, but neither has a row that was checked.
- **"99% of his wealth came after 50 (or 65)."** The sources disagree (95%, 97%, 99%), so the film shows two ages and two numbers and lets them speak: first billion at 55, $143.7 billion at 96.
- **Any claim about his age in 1962.** In January 1962 he was 31 and many outlets say "at 32"; the 1962 stop shows the year only.
